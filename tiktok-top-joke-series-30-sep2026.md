## Universal structure (unchanged from the May series)

| Time | What |
| :-: | :-- |
| 0–2s | Cold open clip — show audio playing, no overlays yet (brand logo lock-up at top only) |
| 2–4s | Punchline lands — score popup punches in BIG over the moment |
| 4–9s | Clip continues / reaction beat / let the viewer recover |
| 9–13s | End card: show name + score reveal + "Fight me." bait |
| 13–15s | Loop hint — hard cut back to the punchline frame |

**Voice rules:**

- No voiceover. Original show audio carries it.
- Burned-in caption of the punchline only. Yellow, big, bottom third.
- Counter bar not used (that's for episode-arc videos).
- Logo lock-up top, @thehumorindex bottom.
- Target 15–18 seconds. Never past 20.

**Per-video workflow:** yt-dlp the clip → send me the punchline timestamp → I render → you schedule in Publer. About 30 min of your time per video.

# After this run

**At day 10 (Sep 17):** rank the first nine by views. Whatever the top three have in common is your format. Weight the back half toward it.

**At day 30 (Oct 7):** you'll have 38 videos of data. Cut to 3/week and rebuild the bank around whatever pattern won. Do not keep grinding daily if the curve is flat.

**Things worth watching for:**

- The airability videos (#21 Curb, #22 Fleabag, #23 Chappelle) are an experiment. "The real #1 is unpostable" is either a strong hook or it reads as a cop-out. Three data points will tell you which.
- The runner-up format (#30–#38) is cheaper to make and reuses fandoms you've already warmed up. If it performs within 30% of the #1 videos, that's your scaling path.
- The deep cuts (Taxi, Larry Sanders, Freaks and Geeks, Fresh Prince) will almost certainly underperform on views. Judge them on follows-per-view instead, not raw views. Niche fandoms convert harder.
